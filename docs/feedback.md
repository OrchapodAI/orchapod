# Feedback for Nebius and NVIDIA

What we hit while building Orchapod on Nebius Token Factory, Nebius AI Cloud and the NVIDIA Nemotron models. Seeded on 2026-10-21 from the docs, the OpenAPI and a rented GPU. On 2026-10-22 two spikes and the first live Agent runs added live Token Factory measurements, and on 2026-10-25 the hosted bring-up and the live golden run added the AI Cloud items. `Source:` says where each finding comes from and where we recorded it. Page paths are as read between 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-22. ## Token Factory

- **Serving a fine-tuned Llama per token: our sources disagreed, and the API settles it with a 410.** On 2026-10-22 `POST /v0/models` returned 410 `{"detail":"LoRA adapter deployment is no longer available"}`, both with an empty body and with the source of a fine-tune that had just succeeded (`<job>:<checkpoint>`, base `meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`); `GET /v0/models` returns 405. No page says so: https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/fine-tuning/models still offers "deploy serverless LoRA adapter models" for Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-3.3-70B, and https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/post-training/models says Meta models deploy only through Dedicated endpoints. A 410 that named the replacement (Dedicated endpoint custom weights, or downloading the adapter) would have saved us a day. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike1/out/v0_models_post_empty.json`, `deploy_result.json`.
- **The deploy page is a 404.** https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/fine-tuning/deploy-custom-model, the page our plan cites for deploying a fine-tune, returned 404, and so does https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/post-training/deploy-custom-model; the how-to-fine-tune page links both (re-checked 2026-10-22). Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-20T22:56Z); https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/post-training/how-to-fine-tune.
- **The live OpenAPI has no `/v0/models`.** Token Factory's OpenAPI (version 20261020-1a55a96cb) does not list the route; it lists `/v0/dedicated_endpoints` (create takes a `custom_weights_id`) and `/v0/dedicated_endpoints/templates`. Source: https://api.tokenfactory.nebius.com/openapi.json, read 2026-10-22.
- **Llama-3.3-70B left Token Factory serverless on 2026-08-31, yet the serverless-LoRA list still names it.** The August 2026 deprecation notice names `meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct` with the date August 31, 2026 and `nvidia/Nemotron-3_5-Lightning` as the replacement, covers the Serverless API and the Playground, and says nothing about LoRA, while https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/fine-tuning/models still lists the 70B as a serverless-LoRA base. On 2026-10-22 the serverless catalog (`GET /v1/models`, 28 models) had no Llama model at all, the 8B included. Source: https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/august-2026-deprecation-notice.md (re-read 2026-10-22); `scripts/spikes/spike1/out/models_verbose.json`.
- **Fine-tuning prices are not public.** The API returns per-token inference prices (`GET /v1/models?verbose=true`, `pricing`) and spec-draft (speculator) training prices (`GET /v1/fine_tuning/models/spec-draft`, e.g. SKU `text_fine_tuning_model_agnostic_training_drafter_small` at 5.0), but no supervised fine-tuning price; the "Token Factory pricing" link on nebius.com/prices opens the console's billing route (tokenfactory.nebius.com/organization/prices), whose page as served carries no prices. So we cannot price a fine-tune before we start it. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike1/out/spec_draft_models.json`; https://nebius.com/prices.
- **Large result files come back as a 307 the OpenAPI (version 20261020-1a55a96cb) does not document.** `GET /v1/files/<id>/content` returned 200 with the bytes for the small checkpoint files and 307 to a presigned `storage.eu-west2.nebius.cloud` URL for `adapter_model.safetensors` (41,994,968 bytes) and `tokenizer.json` (17,209,920 bytes); the OpenAPI lists only 200. A client that does not follow redirects fails after the job has been paid for. `GET /v1/files/<id>/link` returns the same file's URL. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike1/out/content_redirects.json`.
- **A cancel reads as an error.** The cancel reply still says `running`; the job reached `cancelled` about 2.5 minutes later with `error {"code": "1", "message": "error:<ts>:datasets: Finetuning was cancelled"}` and an `error`-level event, and 0 trained tokens. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike1/out/cancel_probe_job.json`, `cancel_probe_events.json`.
- **Custom weights on Dedicated endpoints are beta, on request.** https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/ai-models-inference/dedicated-endpoints/custom-weights says custom weights are currently in beta and available on request. Dedicated endpoints bill per running replica at contract pricing, and zero replicas are not billed. Joining that beta is one of our four fallbacks for a fine-tune that cannot be served per token. Source: that page; the billing terms as recorded in `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-20T22:56Z); `hackathon/STATE.md` (Blockers, fallback c of four).
- **`result_files` download as a PEFT-style LoRA directory, with one naming quirk.** The best checkpoint of our 50-row LoRA job had six files: `adapter_config.json` (peft_type LORA, written by peft 0.19.1, r 8, alpha 8, all seven linear projections, `task_type` null), `adapter_model.safetensors` (448 BF16 tensors), `tokenizer.json`, `tokenizer_config.json`, `chat_template.jinja` and `checkpoint.meta`. The tensor names lack the `base_model.model.` prefix that PEFT's `save_pretrained` writes (`model.layers.0.mlp.down_proj.lora_A.weight`); vLLM v0.18.0 (`parse_fine_tuned_lora_name`) and llama.cpp b11223 (`convert_lora_to_gguf.py`) accept unprefixed names by their source, but no runtime has loaded it yet. TODO(D8): load it in vLLM with `--enable-lora` and in llama.cpp. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike1/out/result_files_meta.json`.
- **Two Token Factory pages name the 8B base differently, and the API takes only one.** https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/post-training/models says `meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`, which `POST /v1/fine_tuning/jobs` refuses with 422 "Model doesn't exist or you don't have access to it"; `meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct` (https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/fine-tuning/models and the OpenAPI's examples, version 20261020-1a55a96cb) works. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike1/out/create_refused_Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.json`, `job_created.json`.
- **Nemotron cannot be fine-tuned on Token Factory.** The fine-tuning list covers Llama 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3, Qwen3 and Qwen2.5, gpt-oss, DeepSeek and Gemma 4. So Lane A tunes a Llama model that Nemotron teaches and judges. Source: `docs/agent_knowledge.md` (Constraints every plan must respect).
- **The reasoning parameter differs between the Nemotron models we served and Token Factory's OpenAPI.** In our vLLM spike, Nemotron Nano 8B v1 accepted the system prompt "detailed thinking off" and the 30B-A3B accepted `enable_thinking=false` in the chat template. Token Factory's OpenAPI (version 20261020) declares `reasoning_effort` (none..max), not `chat_template_kwargs`. Measured 2026-10-22 (spike 2): Token Factory honours both. The same prompt to Nemotron 3 Super took 129 and 139 completion tokens (118 and 128 of them `reasoning_tokens`) in 1.6 and 3.3 s by default, and 7 completion tokens (0 reasoning) in 0.66–0.81 s with either `chat_template_kwargs: {"enable_thinking": false}` or `reasoning_effort: "none"`; Lightning and Ultra also gave 0 reasoning tokens with the first. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike2/results.jsonl` (rows "reasoning …"); `containers/unsloth-train/spike/RESULTS.md` (both completions); Token Factory's OpenAPI (`reasoning_effort`), as noted in `hackathon/briefs/2026-10-20-contracts-platform.md` (answer 3, Open questions 4).
- **Nemotron 3.5 Lightning puts its default reasoning in `content`; Super keeps it out.** With no reasoning switch, Lightning's `content` began "Here's a thinking process:" and ran to `max_tokens` (1,200 tokens, all counted as `reasoning_tokens`, `finish_reason: length`), while `reasoning` and `reasoning_content` were null. Super, by default, returns its reasoning twice, in `reasoning` and in `reasoning_content`, and `content` holds only the answer. A Lightning call without the switch therefore gets its thinking back as the answer. Measured 2026-10-22 (spike 2). Source: `scripts/spikes/spike2/results.jsonl` (rows "report Lightning, reasoning default" and "reasoning default #1").
- **Lightning was slow and erratic on 2026-10-22 (03:14–03:32Z), while Super, Ultra and Nano answered in under 2.2 s.** A 7-token answer to a 113-token prompt took 1.0–37.2 s over 10 calls; a 1,211-token report prompt took 16–110 s over 5 answered calls, and a sixth timed out at 120 s four times in a row (490 s, no answer). At 03:31–03:33Z, while Lightning still took 16–18 s for the 7-token answer, the report prompt took 2.1 s on Super and 1.6 s on Nano, and the judge prompt 0.7 s on Ultra. Our traffic was one call at a time, far below Lightning's listed 400k tokens and 600 requests per minute. The replies' `created` times show each request arrived at once, so the wait was inside Token Factory. Because of it, Nemotron 3 Super now writes our run reports instead of Lightning. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike2/results.jsonl` (rows "report …", "quality-filter shape …", "repeat …", "alt …"); the ruling in `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-22T03:40Z).
- **Token Factory's OpenAPI and its JSON page disagree about `response_format`.** The OpenAPI (version 20261020) description says only `json_object` or `text` is supported, while https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/ai-models-inference/json documents `json_schema`, which our planner and our synthetic-data node depend on. Measured 2026-10-22 (spike 2): the live API follows the docs page. `response_format` `json_schema` with `strict: true` returned 200 and was enforced on Super: the reply used exactly the keys and enum of a schema the prompt never mentioned, and the prompt counted 22 tokens, so the schema was not added to it. It also held on Lightning (an integer enum, 10 of 10 calls) and on Ultra (an untyped enum), and the planner's schema (an open `inputs` object, an untyped enum) came back valid, with `inputs` filled. Super's `system_fingerprint` names a vLLM build (Lightning's is null). `json_object` plus the schema in the prompt was valid too on a four-field schema, but there the prompt, not the decoder, holds the shape. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike2/results.jsonl` (rows "schema …", "planner …", "judge …"); Token Factory's OpenAPI, version 20261020 (`response_format` description), as recorded in `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-21T01:15Z) and `hackathon/briefs/2026-10-20-contracts-platform.md` (header); https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/ai-models-inference/json; `docs/contracts.md` (TokenFactory, `chat`).
- **`GET /v1/models?verbose=true` is the price list, with limits and quantization, but it omits structured output.** Each model carries `pricing.prompt` and `pricing.completion` in $ per token (per 1M: Super 0.30/0.90, Ultra 1.00/3.00, Lightning and Nano 0.06/0.24, matching our table), `input_cache_read` (Super 0.06, Ultra 0.12 per 1M; null for Lightning and Nano), `per_request_limits` (Super and Ultra 200k tokens and 300 requests a minute, Lightning 400k and 600, Nano 800k and 100) and `quantization` (Super fp4, Ultra fp4, Lightning bf16, Nano fp8). `supported_features` lists only `tools` and `reasoning`, though `json_schema` works. Usage reports cached prompt tokens (`prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens`: 16 of 47 on a repeated prompt), so a repeated prefix bills below list price; our client bills every prompt token at list price, an over-count. Measured 2026-10-22 (spike 2), free call. Source: `scripts/spikes/spike2/results.jsonl` (first row; `usage` of rows "reasoning …").
- **Nemotron model ids differ in capitalisation and in how they write 3.5.** `nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b`, `nvidia/Nemotron-3-Ultra-550b-a55b`, `nvidia/Nemotron-3_5-Lightning`, `nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B` and `nvidia/Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL` were the ids live on 2026-10-20. Source: `hackathon/ORCHESTRATOR.md` (section 7); `docs/contracts.md` (TokenFactory ids); `docs/agent_knowledge.md` (model table).
- **Super's live plans differ from our templates.** On 2026-10-22, Nemotron 3 Super's live plan for intent #3 left out GSM8K's dataset config, which our template sets, so data-prep failed after 73 s (run `cf49d0f1`, $0.0034). We hardened data-prep, which now picks `main` (else the first config) when a dataset has several, and logs a warning. The planner's knowledge text now names `hf_config`. The rerun passed (run `59786797`). We take it as an honest lesson in planner robustness: a plan can match the JSON schema and still leave out what a node needs. Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-22T07:30Z and 07:34Z).

## Nebius AI Cloud

- **Job logs are not available over REST.** Serverless Job logs come from the CLI (`nebius ai job logs <id> --follow`) or the console. A node running on a Job therefore cannot be followed through the API, so our nodes post their logs to the control plane, which must be reachable at a public https URL (ngrok in development). Source: https://docs.nebius.com/serverless/quickstart/jobs and https://docs.nebius.com/cli/reference/ai/job/create, as recorded in `hackathon/PLAN.md` (Serverless AI Jobs row); `hackathon/ORCHESTRATOR.md` (section 7); `docs/contracts.md` (NebiusServerless).
- **A Job's timeout defaults to 24 hours.** It can be set from 1 h to 168 h, so we set 3600 s ourselves; left unset, a stuck Job would run for a day. Source: `hackathon/PLAN.md` (Serverless AI Jobs row); `docs/contracts.md` (NebiusServerless, `jobs.create`).
- **No AI Cloud credit is published for the hackathon.** The listed credits are $25 and $25 for Token Factory and $25 for Tavily; GPU time is out of pocket and needs a project with billing and a GPU quota. Our eu-north1 project had H100 ×8 and L40S ×8 quota when we checked on 2026-10-25. Source: `hackathon/PLAN.md` (Credits available now), citing https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/resources and https://dev.nebius.com/builders; `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T02:22Z).
- **Serverless AI's own GPU-hour rate is unverified.** We have only the published GPU-hour list rates; whether Serverless Jobs and Endpoints bill at them is unconfirmed. Our meter prices the golden run's Job at list: $0.8408 for 11.2 min of H100. TODO(D8): the rate on Nebius's bill. Source: `hackathon/briefs/2026-10-20-contracts-platform.md` (Open questions 2); `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T06:42Z).
- **Creates answered PermissionDenied after billing activation, until a CLI re-login.** Before billing was active, every AI Cloud create (a VM, a bucket) answered PermissionDenied. After activation the same creates still did, retried every minute for 10 minutes. They went through once the CLI's federation token was refreshed after activation. The error said only PermissionDenied. An error that named the cause, or a docs line saying to log in again after activating billing, would have saved us time. Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T02:22Z, 02:57Z and 05:02Z).
- **The non-GPU vCPU quota is 0 by default in eu-north1.** Our project there had GPU quota but no vCPUs for an ordinary VM, so the hosting VM went to eu-west1, while the bucket and the GPUs stayed in eu-north1. A default above 0, or a note on the quota page, would have kept everything in one region. Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T02:22Z).
- **Serverless Jobs pull from a Container Registry in the same project with no credentials.** Our GitHub images were private, so we pushed the node images to a Nebius Container Registry in the same project. The H100 Job pulled the 44 GB training image with no credentials, as the docs say. This worked. Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T05:24Z and 06:02Z).
- **`nebius ai job logs` caps `--tail` at 1000.** Job logs have no REST route, so a failed Job's traceback comes from this command. A higher cap, or paging, would show a long log in full. Source: `hackathon/briefs/2026-10-25-wave6.md` (devrel-w9, item 2); `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T06:02Z).
- **The Nebius MCP server fails to start with pydantic 2.14.** `nebius-mcp-server` pins mcp ≤ 1.16.0, and uv resolved pydantic 2.14.1 for it, so the server failed on import. Running it with `uvx --with "pydantic<2.12"` fixed it. An upper bound on pydantic in the package, or a newer mcp, would avoid it. Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T00:12Z).
- **Image pulls: about 6 min, then 4.6 min.** The H100 Job pulled our 44 GB image (state IMAGE_PULLING) in about 6 min on the first live golden run and in 4.6 min on the second. Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T06:02Z and 08:59Z).
- **An endpoint starts in about 4 min.** An L40S endpoint went from PROVISIONING to RUNNING in about 4 min on both live runs. Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T07:03Z and 08:59Z).
- **Deleting an endpoint at its expiry worked on both runs.** Our API's TTL deleted each run's endpoint when its keep-alive ended (the second at 09:12:20Z). Source: `hackathon/LOG.md` (2026-10-25T06:58Z); `hackathon/briefs/2026-10-25-devrel-w10.md` (New facts).

## NVIDIA Nemotron models

The numbers below come from one spike on a rented vast.ai A100 80 GB, outside Nebius (the first 200 rows of yahma/alpaca-cleaned, 60 steps). They are inputs to a decision, not claims about Nebius.

- **Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B-v1 was an easy LoRA student.** With Unsloth 16-bit LoRA, 60 steps took 62.5 s at a 15.9 GiB peak, the merge to 16-bit took 40.5 s (15 GB) and vLLM 0.18.0 was healthy 51 s after start. It needed no extra kernels or dependency pins. Source: `containers/unsloth-train/spike/RESULTS.md` (Main run); `hackathon/briefs/2026-10-20-ml-engineering-spike4-report.md` (DECISION).
- **The 30B-A3B hybrid (Mamba and MoE) passed train, merge and serve, with more work than the 8B.** PEFT cannot wrap the router, a raw `nn.Parameter`, so it stayed frozen, and PEFT 0.21 refuses LoRA on Mamba `out_proj` and `conv1d` for `nemotron_h`. The 128 routed experts stayed frozen because of our choice of LoRA targets (attention, Mamba `in_proj` and the shared experts). Installing `mamba_ssm` pulls `tilelang`, which downgrades `apache-tvm-ffi` and breaks vLLM's xgrammar import. The Hugging Face remote code loops over the experts, so training ran about 11 s a step against 1 s for the 8B, with the GPU about 38% busy, and peaked at 60.5 GiB. Source: `containers/unsloth-train/spike/RESULTS.md` (Stretch, Surprises 2 to 4).
